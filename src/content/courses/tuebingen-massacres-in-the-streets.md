title: "Massacres in the Streets: Religious Violence in Early Modern Europe"

start: 2024-04-01
format: Seminar, designed and taught
---
This course will consider the role and impact of religious violence in early modern Europe. The introduction and spread of Protestantism challenged the traditional Catholic culture and society of Europe. The increasing influence of Protestants resulted in numerous clashes across the continent, with France arguably witnessing the most violence. The severe escalation of violence resulted in an upheaval of religious, political, social, and cultural institutions across Europe, and resulted in different attitudes as Europeans embarked on the Age of Discovery in the Americas.

The seminars will allow students to examine the causes and results of religious violence in the early modern period. By focusing on more broad episodes, such as the French Wars of Religion and the Thirty Years War, and more localised episodes, like the 1562 Massacre of Sens, 1641 Irish Rebellion, the 1572 St. Bartholomew’s Day Massacre, and the 1647 Revolt of Masaniello, the students will gain a comprehensive understanding of the different aspects of religious violence, such as iconoclasm and ‘rites of violence’. The seminar will be based on visual and written primary sources, along with the secondary sources from historians such as Benjamin Kaplan. The module would also benefit from the sources and expertise of Tübingen’s Centre for Religion, Culture, and Society. By focusing on the instigations, acts, and outcomes of violence in early modern Europe, students will gain a solid understanding on how religious violence shaped Europe during this time, and how religious violence continues to play a role in modern day conflicts.
