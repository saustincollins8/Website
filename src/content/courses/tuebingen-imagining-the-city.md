---
title: "Imagining the City: Urban Spaces in the Global Early Modern Period"
institution: "Eberhard Karls Universität Tübingen "
when: Winter Semester 2024–25
start: 2024-04-01
format: Seminar, designed and taught
---
The early modern period witnessed an increasingly connected world, with Europeans interacting with people and urban spaces from Asia, Africa, and the newly ‘discovered’ Americas. The increasing connections with other peoples contributed to the growing importance of cities during this time, as the urban spaces became more populated and specialised in different economic functions. Combined with the religious turbulence experienced by mostly Catholics and Protestants, urban spaces experienced an increase in functionality, contestation, and violence.

The seminars will challenge students to ‘imagine’ how different cities functioned during this time, and how the different religious, political, royal, and economic influences shaped the urban space of the highlighted cities. Seminars will be thematic and draw examples from a number of highlighted cities. Some examples will be royal, print, trade, Catholic, port, and Reformation cities. Examples of highlighted cities will be Lyon, Constantinople, Geneva, Venice, Goa, Edo, Manila, Cuzco, Beijing, and Benguela. Each seminar will see a discussion concerning the primary and secondary sources of each thematic city. Primary sources will include visual and written sources, whilst a possible excursion to the altstädte of Tübingen or Esslingen can emphasise the seminar discussions concerning how we can analyse and challenge our interpretation of cities on a global scale in the early modern period.
