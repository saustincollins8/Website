Despite spending several years in the British and German university systems, which reflect my adaptability and willingness to experience new systems and environments, I still firmly appreciate the small group settings my undergraduate experience at a Liberal Arts college offered. The opportunity to discuss a wide range of topics and ideas with a diverse group of students created a well-rounded and inclusive environment. This is the same environment that I strive to create in my professional career as an educator. 

The pedological approach of Socratic teaching has remained the base of my teaching style as I have transitioned into the American university system. Conducting analytical and philosophical discussions with students, which often revolve around historiographical trends and their relevance to our contemporary times, has enabled the Liberal Arts tradition to resonate within my classroom environment, whilst simultaneously allowing other teaching philosophies in my class discussions. 

Given my excitement for encouraging students' interests, even when they are not History majors (as in the general survey courses I have taught), bringing in different perspectives and source materials while still grounding them in discussion-based classes has proven to be an effective methodology. The inclusion of Socratic discussion in all my classes has been reinforced by using a variety of resources to help students gain a more complete understanding of the course materials. I frequently employ visual sources, such as maps, paintings, photographs, newsreels, literature (novels and poetry), and I also place an emphasis on primary sources in all classes.
